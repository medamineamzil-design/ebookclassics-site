About the book

The Great Gatsby is narrated by Nick Carraway, a Yale-educated Midwesterner who moves to Long Island in the summer of 1922 to work in bonds and rents a modest house next to the mansion of the mysterious, fabulously wealthy Jay Gatsby, famous for throwing enormous, extravagant parties that he himself rarely appears to enjoy. Nick reconnects with his cousin Daisy Buchanan, married to the brutish, unfaithful Tom Buchanan across the bay, and gradually learns that Gatsby's entire, meticulously constructed life, the mansion, the parties, the invented persona of effortless old wealth, has been built for one purpose: to win back Daisy, whom he loved and lost as a poor young army officer five years earlier, before amassing his fortune, implied to be through bootlegging and other illicit dealings, specifically in hopes of becoming someone she could marry. Nick arranges a reunion, and Gatsby and Daisy begin a secret affair, Gatsby convinced that if he simply provides enough wealth and spectacle he can erase the intervening years entirely and recreate their past exactly as it was. Tom, threatened both by Gatsby's growing claim on Daisy and by his own guilt over an ongoing affair with Myrtle Wilson, the wife of a struggling garage owner, confronts Gatsby directly during a tense gathering at the Plaza Hotel, exposing the criminal source of his wealth and forcing Daisy to choose between the two men. Daisy, unable to fully commit to Gatsby's demand that she declare she never loved Tom, wavers, and the group drives back separately; Daisy, driving Gatsby's car with Gatsby beside her, strikes and kills Myrtle Wilson, who had run into the road believing the car was Tom's. Gatsby, still devoted, resolves to take the blame for Daisy, but Tom tells Myrtle's grief-stricken, distraught husband George that the car belongs to Gatsby, allowing George to wrongly conclude Gatsby was both Myrtle's killer and her lover. George shoots Gatsby dead in his own pool before killing himself. In the novel's bleak coda, almost no one attends Gatsby's funeral, Daisy and Tom retreat without consequence into their wealth, and Nick, disillusioned by the moral emptiness he has witnessed, returns to the Midwest, reflecting on Gatsby's tragic, doomed faith in the American capacity to repeat and perfect the past.

About F. Scott Fitzgerald

American novelist and short-story writer (1896–1940), a leading figure of the Jazz Age, best known for The Great Gatsby.

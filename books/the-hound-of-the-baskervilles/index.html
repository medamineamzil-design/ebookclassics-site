About the book

The Hound of the Baskervilles opens with Dr. Mortimer visiting Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson at Baker Street to relate a chilling family legend: generations ago, a Baskerville ancestor's cruelty summoned a spectral, monstrous hound that has haunted and killed the family's heirs ever since. The immediate trigger is the sudden death of Sir Charles Baskerville, found near his estate on the Devon moor with an expression of terror and, more troublingly, enormous paw prints nearby. The new heir, Sir Henry Baskerville, has just arrived from Canada, and Mortimer fears the legend will claim him too, especially after Sir Henry receives an anonymous warning note and has a boot stolen from his hotel. Holmes, tied up with another case in London, sends Watson to Dartmoor with Sir Henry to investigate and protect him, while promising to follow. Watson's narration, largely delivered through his letters and diary entries, catalogs the moor's bleak, fog-shrouded atmosphere, the eccentric neighbors, the naturalist Stapleton and his beautiful sister, the reclusive Mr. Frankland, and an escaped convict loose on the moor, and reports a series of unsettling incidents, ghostly cries at night, a mysterious figure spotted silhouetted against the moon. Watson eventually discovers that the silhouetted stranger is in fact Holmes himself, who arrived secretly earlier to investigate unobserved and has been camped in a stone hut, letting Watson's official investigation serve as cover. Holmes reveals that Stapleton, the seemingly friendly naturalist, is actually a disinherited Baskerville relative plotting to eliminate Sir Henry and claim the estate, using a real, painted-with-phosphorus dog to exploit the family legend and terrify his victims to death or drive them into fatal accidents on the treacherous moor. In the climax, Holmes and Watson lie in wait as Stapleton sets the luminous hound on Sir Henry, shooting the beast down just in time; Stapleton himself later drowns while fleeing into the treacherous Grimpen Mire, and the supernatural legend is revealed to be entirely the product of human greed and cunning stagecraft.

About Arthur Conan Doyle

British writer and physician (1859–1930), creator of the detective Sherlock Holmes, one of the most influential figures in crime fiction.

How it begins

Mr. Sherlock Holmes, who was usually very late in the mornings, save upon those not infrequent occasions when he was up all night, was seated at the breakfast table. I stood upon the hearth-rug and picked up the stick which our visitor had left behind him the night before. It was a fine, thick piece of wood, bulbous-headed, of the sort which is known as a “Penang lawyer.” Just under the head was a broad silver band nearly an inch across. “To James Mortimer, M.R.C.S., from his friends of the C.C.H.,” was engraved upon it, with the date “1884.” It was just such a stick as the old-fashioned family practitioner used to carry—dignified, solid, and reassuring.

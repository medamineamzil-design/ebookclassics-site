About the book

Jane Eyre traces its narrator's life from a miserable, loveless childhood as an orphaned dependent in her cruel aunt's household at Gateshead, through her harsh schooling at Lowood, an institution whose punitive regime and typhus outbreak claim the life of her closest friend, Helen Burns, and shape Jane's lifelong resistance to hypocrisy and injustice. At eighteen, having become a capable teacher, Jane takes a position as governess at Thornfield Hall, tutoring the ward of the brooding, unconventional Edward Rochester. Despite a stark difference in social position, Jane and Rochester develop a relationship built on genuine intellectual and emotional equality, conveyed through blunt, searching conversations rather than conventional courtship. Thornfield is haunted by strange occurrences, unexplained fires, a violent attack on a house guest, that Jane cannot account for. Rochester proposes marriage, and Jane accepts, but on their wedding day the ceremony is interrupted by the revelation that Rochester is already married: his wife, Bertha Mason, a Creole woman from Jamaica who has gone violently insane, is confined in the attic, and it is she who has been responsible for Thornfield's disturbances. Devastated but unwilling to become Rochester's mistress despite his pleading, Jane flees penniless across the moors, nearly dying before being taken in by the Rivers siblings, who turn out to be her cousins and, through an inherited fortune from an uncle, make her financially independent for the first time in her life. St. John Rivers, a cold, zealously religious clergyman, proposes a loveless marriage as a missionary partnership in India, and Jane nearly accepts out of duty before a mysterious, telepathic call, hearing Rochester's voice crying her name across the distance, sends her back to Thornfield. She finds the house burned to the ground by Bertha, who died in the fire, and Rochester blinded and maimed while attempting to save his household. Now Jane's equal in dependency rather than his social inferior, she marries him, and the novel closes with their union restored on terms of genuine mutual need rather than unequal power.

About Charlotte Brontë

English novelist and poet (1816–1855), eldest of the Brontë sisters, best known for the novel Jane Eyre.

How it begins

There was no possibility of taking a walk that day. We had been wandering, indeed, in the leafless shrubbery an hour in the morning; but since dinner (Mrs. Reed, when there was no company, dined early) the cold winter wind had brought with it clouds so sombre, and a rain so penetrating, that further outdoor exercise was now out of the question.

I was glad of it: I never liked long walks, especially on chilly afternoons: dreadful to me was the coming home in the raw twilight, with nipped fingers and toes, and a heart saddened by the chidings of Bessie, the nurse, and humbled by the consciousness of my physical inferiority to Eliza, John, and Georgiana Reed.

The said Eliza, John, and Georgiana were now clustered round their mama in the drawing-room: she lay reclined on a sofa by the fireside, and with her darlings about her (for the time neither quarrelling nor crying) looked perfectly happy. Me, she had dispensed from joining the group; saying, “She regretted to be under the necessity of keeping me at a distance; but that until she heard from Bessie, and could discover by her own observation, that I was endeavouring in good earnest to acquire a more sociable and childlike disposition, a more attractive and sprightly manner—something lighter, franker, more natural, as it were—she really must exclude me from privileges intended only for contented, happy, little children.”

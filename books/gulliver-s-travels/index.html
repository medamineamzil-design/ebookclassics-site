About the book

Lemuel Gulliver, a ship's surgeon with a taste for travel, tells the story of four extraordinary voyages. Shipwrecked first in Lilliput, he finds himself a giant among people only a few inches tall, caught up in their petty quarrels and wars. In Brobdingnag the situation is reversed and he becomes a tiny curiosity among giants. His third journey takes him to the flying island of Laputa and neighbouring lands full of impractical scientists and projectors, and his fourth to a country ruled by the Houyhnhnms, rational horses, who share their land with brutish humanlike creatures called Yahoos.

On the surface the book is a lively adventure parodying the travel writing popular in Swift's day, which is why it has long been enjoyed by children in shortened forms. Underneath it is a sharp satire of politics, religious disputes, science, war and human pride. Changes of scale let Swift show human behaviour from new and unflattering angles. Its plain, deadpan style makes the absurdities funnier and the criticism harder to escape, and its questions about reason and human nature remain unsettling.

Swift, an Anglo-Irish clergyman who was Dean of St Patrick's Cathedral in Dublin, published the book anonymously in London in 1726. It was an immediate success. A revised edition appeared in Dublin in 1735. Words such as Lilliputian entered the language, and the book has been adapted many times for film, television and the stage.

About Jonathan Swift

Jonathan Swift was born in Dublin in 1667 to English parents; his father died before he was born. He studied at Trinity College Dublin and then worked as secretary to the statesman Sir William Temple in England. He was ordained in the Church of Ireland and became a leading political writer for the Tory government in London. In 1713 he was appointed Dean of St Patrick's Cathedral in Dublin, a post he held until his death. He died in Dublin in 1745.

Swift's early satire A Tale of a Tub mocked religious extremes. His most famous book, Gulliver's Travels, published in 1726, sends its hero to Lilliput, Brobdingnag, Laputa and the land of the rational horses called Houyhnhnms, satirising politics, science and human pride. A Modest Proposal, from 1729, ironically suggests that the poor of Ireland sell their children as food, a savage attack on the treatment of the Irish poor. Swift is considered the foremost prose satirist in English.

How it begins

The author gives some account of himself and family. His first inducements to travel. He is shipwrecked, and swims for his life, gets safe on shore in the country of Lilliput; is made a prisoner, and carried up the country.

My father had a small estate in Nottinghamshire; I was the third of five sons. He sent me to Emanuel College in Cambridge at fourteen years old, where I resided three years, and applied myself close to my studies; but the charge of maintaining me, although I had a very scanty allowance, being too great for a narrow fortune, I was bound apprentice to Mr. James Bates, an eminent surgeon in London, with whom I continued four years. My father now and then sending me small sums of money, I laid them out in learning navigation, and other parts of the mathematics, useful to those who intend to travel, as I always believed it would be, some time or other, my fortune to do. When I left Mr. Bates, I went down to my father: where, by the assistance of him and my uncle John, and some other relations, I got forty pounds, and a promise of thirty pounds a year to maintain me at Leyden: there I studied physic two years and seven months, knowing it would be useful in long voyages.

Soon after my return from Leyden, I was recommended by my good master, Mr. Bates, to be surgeon to the Swallow, Captain Abraham Pannel, commander; with whom I continued three years and a half, making a voyage or two into the Levant, and some other parts. When I came back I resolved to settle in London; to which Mr. Bates, my master, encouraged me, and by him I was recommended to several patients. I took part of a small house in the Old Jewry; and being advised to alter my condition, I married Mrs. Mary Burton, second daughter to Mr. Edmund Burton, hosier, in Newgate-street, with whom I received four hundred pounds for a portion.

But my good master Bates dying in two years after, and I having few friends, my business began to fail; for my conscience would not suffer me to imitate the bad practice of too many among my brethren. Having therefore consulted with my wife, and some of my acquaintance, I determined to go again to sea. I was surgeon successively in two ships, and made several voyages, for six years, to the East and West Indies, by which I got some addition to my fortune. My hours of leisure I spent in reading the best authors, ancient and modern, being always provided with a good number of books; and when I was ashore, in observing the manners and dispositions of the people, as well as learning their language; wherein I had a great facility, by the strength of my memory.

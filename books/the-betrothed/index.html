About the book

The Betrothed is set in Lombardy between 1628 and 1630, when the region was under Spanish rule. Renzo and Lucia, two young silk workers from a village near Lake Como, are about to be married when the local priest, Don Abbondio, refuses to perform the ceremony. Two armed thugs have warned him off on behalf of Don Rodrigo, a local lord who has set his sights on Lucia. Separated, the couple are swept into the wider currents of their age: Lucia seeks refuge in a convent at Monza, Renzo is caught up in bread riots in Milan, and both meet figures such as the courageous friar Cristoforo and a feared, powerful nobleman known only as the Unnamed.

Manzoni combines a love story with a vast portrait of society showing how ordinary people suffer under injustice, famine, war and plague. The novel is deeply concerned with conscience, faith and providence, but it is also full of irony and humour.

First published in 1827, the novel was revised by Manzoni over the following years and republished in 1840 to 1842 in language closer to the Italian spoken in Florence, making it a model for a national language. It is widely regarded as the most important Italian novel of the nineteenth century and is studied in Italian schools. Verdi composed his Requiem in Manzoni's memory, and the book has been adapted for film, television and stage.

First published in Italian as I promessi sposi in 1827; considered the most influential novel in the Italian language.

About Alessandro Manzoni

Alessandro Manzoni was born in Milan in 1785. His mother was the daughter of the Enlightenment jurist Cesare Beccaria, and as a young man he lived with her in Paris among liberal intellectuals. After his marriage to Enrichetta Blondel he returned to the Catholic faith, a change that shaped all his later writing. He wrote sacred hymns, historical tragedies and a celebrated ode on Napoleon's death. Honoured as a national figure, he was made a senator in 1860 and died in Milan in 1873.

Manzoni's masterpiece, The Betrothed, first appeared in 1827. Set in Lombardy in the 1620s, it follows two young villagers, Renzo and Lucia, whose marriage is blocked by a powerful nobleman, through famine, revolt and plague. Manzoni revised the language toward educated Florentine speech, and the final 1840 edition helped shape modern standard Italian. Its blend of history, faith, humour and compassion for ordinary people has made it a fixture of Italian schools.

How it begins

That branch of the Lake of Como, which turns toward the south between two unbroken chains of mountains, presenting to the eye a succession of bays and gulfs, formed by their jutting and retiring ridges, suddenly contracts itself between a headland to the right and an extended sloping bank on the left, and assumes the flow and appearance of a river. The bridge by which the two shores are here united, appears to render the transformation more apparent, and marks the point at which the lake ceases, and the Adda recommences, to resume, however, the name of Lake where the again receding banks allow the water to expand itself anew into bays and gulfs. The bank, formed by the deposit of three large mountain streams, descends from the bases of two contiguous mountains, the one called St. Martin, the other by a Lombard name, Resegone, from its long line of summits, which in truth give it the appearance of a saw; so that there is no one who would not at first sight, especially viewing it in front, from the ramparts of Milan that face the north, at once distinguish it in all that extensive range from other mountains of less name and more ordinary form. The bank, for a considerable distance, rises with a gentle and continual ascent, then breaks into hills and hollows, rugged or level land, according to the formation of the mountain rocks, and the action of the floods.

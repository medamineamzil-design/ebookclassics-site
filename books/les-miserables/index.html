About the book

First published in French in 1862, in five volumes.

Les Misérables follows Jean Valjean, a former convict imprisoned nineteen years for stealing bread, across several decades of nineteenth-century France as he struggles to escape both poverty and the relentless pursuit of the law. Transformed by the unexpected mercy of a bishop who gives him stolen silver rather than turning him in, Valjean breaks his parole, assumes a new identity, and eventually becomes a wealthy factory owner and respected mayor under the name Madeleine, using his fortune for charity. He is dogged throughout by Inspector Javert, a police officer whose rigid, unbending belief in law and order makes him incapable of recognizing redemption or circumstance, only guilt and its punishment. When a innocent man is mistakenly arrested as the real Valjean, the mayor confesses his true identity rather than let another suffer in his place, sacrificing his security. He then dedicates himself to caring for Fantine, a dying factory worker who was dismissed and driven to prostitution after concealing an illegitimate daughter, Cosette, who is being cruelly exploited by the corrupt innkeepers the Thénardiers. Valjean rescues Cosette, raises her as his own, and the two live in hiding in Paris and a convent to escape Javert's pursuit. Years later, as a young woman, Cosette falls in love with Marius, an idealistic student drawn into the republican uprising of the 1832 Paris rebellion, staged around a doomed barricade. Valjean, initially wary of losing Cosette, ultimately risks his life during the uprising, saving the wounded Marius by carrying him through the Paris sewers. Javert, captured by the rebels and spared execution by Valjean himself, is so shattered by this act of mercy from a man he has hunted for decades, mercy that contradicts his entire worldview, that he takes his own life. Valjean, having secured Cosette and Marius's marriage and happiness, withdraws from their lives out of guilt over his past, growing weaker until the couple discovers the truth and rushes to his side as he dies, at peace, surrounded by love he long believed himself unworthy of.

About Victor Hugo

French novelist, poet, and playwright (1802-1885), author of Les Misérables and The Hunchback of Notre-Dame, a leading figure of French Romanticism.

True or false, that which is said of men often occupies as important a place in their lives, and above all in their destinies, as that which they do. M. Myriel was the son of a councillor of the Parliament of Aix; hence he belonged to the nobility of the bar. It was said that his father, destining him to be the heir of his own post, had married him at a very early age, eighteen or twenty, in accordance with a custom which is rather widely prevalent in parliamentary families. In spite of this marriage, however, it was said that Charles Myriel created a great deal of talk. He was well formed, though rather short in stature, elegant, graceful, intelligent; the whole of the first portion of his life had been devoted to the world and to gallantry.

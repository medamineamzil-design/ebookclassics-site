About the book

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz follows Dorothy Gale, a young girl on a Kansas farm, whose house is lifted by a cyclone and deposited in the magical land of Oz, landing directly on and killing the Wicked Witch of the East. Freed of the witch's tyranny, the local Munchkins and the Good Witch of the North give Dorothy the dead witch's enchanted silver shoes and direct her to follow the yellow brick road to the Emerald City, where the powerful Wizard of Oz might be able to help her return home to Kansas. Along the way she gathers companions, each seeking something they believe they lack: a Scarecrow who wants a brain, believing himself foolish despite frequently proving clever; a Tin Woodman who wants a heart, despite acting with consistent compassion and grief throughout the journey; and a Cowardly Lion who wants courage, despite repeatedly acting bravely to protect his friends. The group faces a series of dangers, deadly poppy fields that induce magical sleep, hostile trees, fighting Kalidahs, before reaching the Emerald City, where the Wizard, appearing to each visitor in a different terrifying or awe-inspiring form, agrees to grant their wishes only if they first destroy the Wicked Witch of the West, ruler of the western quadrant of Oz. The witch, alerted to their approach, attacks them with wolves, crows, bees, and finally her Winged Monkeys, who capture Dorothy and destroy the Tin Woodman and Scarecrow, though Dorothy survives and, in a moment of desperate anger, throws a bucket of water on the witch, who melts away entirely, a weakness no one had previously discovered. Returning triumphant to the Emerald City, the companions discover the Wizard is not a powerful sorcerer at all but an ordinary man from Omaha who arrived in Oz by hot air balloon and has ruled through elaborate illusion and humbug. Unable to literally grant magical gifts, he instead gives the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion symbolic tokens, a diploma, a heart-shaped clock, a medal, that let them recognize qualities they possessed all along, then attempts to fly Dorothy home in his balloon, though she is accidentally left behind when it departs without her. Ultimately Glinda the Good Witch of the South reveals that Dorothy's silver shoes could have taken her home at any time, and Dorothy clicks her heels together and returns to Kansas.

About L. Frank Baum

American author (1856-1919), best known for creating The Wonderful Wizard of Oz and its many sequels.

How it begins

Dorothy lived in the midst of the great Kansas prairies, with Uncle Henry, who was a farmer, and Aunt Em, who was the farmer’s wife. Their house was small, for the lumber to build it had to be carried by wagon many miles. There were four walls, a floor and a roof, which made one room; and this room contained a rusty looking cookstove, a cupboard for the dishes, a table, three or four chairs, and the beds. Uncle Henry and Aunt Em had a big bed in one corner, and Dorothy a little bed in another corner. There was no garret at all, and no cellar—except a small hole dug in the ground, called a cyclone cellar, where the family could go in case one of those great whirlwinds arose, mighty enough to crush any building in its path. It was reached by a trap door in the middle of the floor, from which a ladder led down into the small, dark hole.

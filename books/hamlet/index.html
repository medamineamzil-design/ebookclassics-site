About the book

Hamlet opens on the battlements of Elsinore Castle in Denmark, where guards have twice seen the ghost of the recently deceased King Hamlet and summon his son, Prince Hamlet, to witness it. The ghost reveals to Hamlet that he was murdered by his own brother, Claudius, who poured poison in his ear while he slept, and that Claudius has since married Hamlet's mother, Queen Gertrude, and seized the throne; the ghost demands revenge. Hamlet, horrified but also paralyzed by doubt about the ghost's reliability and the morality of the act demanded of him, feigns madness to buy time and observe Claudius, a performance so convincing it destabilizes the entire court, including his relationship with Ophelia, the woman who loves him and whose father, the meddling courtier Polonius, believes Hamlet's madness stems from thwarted love. To test the ghost's claim, Hamlet arranges for a troupe of traveling actors to perform a play closely mirroring his father's murder, watching Claudius's reaction; the king's visible horror and abrupt departure convinces Hamlet the ghost told the truth. Hamlet nearly kills Claudius in prayer immediately afterward but stays his hand, reasoning that killing a praying man would send his soul to heaven rather than damnation, a decision that sets in motion the play's mounting catastrophe. Hamlet then mistakenly kills Polonius, hidden behind a tapestry in Gertrude's chamber, believing him to be Claudius; this act drives Ophelia into genuine madness and eventually suicide by drowning, and turns her brother Laertes into Hamlet's sworn enemy. Claudius, now openly plotting against Hamlet, arranges his exile to England with a secret order for his execution, which Hamlet discovers and evades, returning to Denmark. Claudius then conspires with the grieving, furious Laertes to kill Hamlet in a rigged fencing match, using both a poisoned blade and a poisoned cup of wine as backup. In the catastrophic final scene, Gertrude accidentally drinks the poisoned wine and dies; Laertes wounds Hamlet with the poisoned blade before the swords are swapped and Hamlet wounds Laertes with it in turn; the dying Laertes confesses the plot; Hamlet, finally acting decisively, stabs Claudius and forces the poisoned wine on him as well. Hamlet himself dies moments later, naming the Norwegian prince Fortinbras as his successor, and the play closes with Fortinbras arriving to claim a throne surrounded by corpses.

About William Shakespeare

English playwright and poet (1564-1616), widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language, author of plays such as Hamlet and Romeo and Juliet.

MARCELLUS. Horatio says ’tis but our fantasy, And will not let belief take hold of him Touching this dreaded sight, twice seen of us. Therefore I have entreated him along With us to watch the minutes of this night, That if again this apparition come He may approve our eyes and speak to it.

HORATIO. Tush, tush, ’twill not appear.

BARNARDO. Sit down awhile, And let us once again assail your ears, That are so fortified against our story, What we two nights have seen.
